# Felicity writing rule

Apply this rule when drafting or revising prose for a person or a language model.

## Priorities

Use this order when goals conflict:

1. Preserve truth and intended meaning.
2. Perform the text's actual job.
3. Fit the reader and context.
4. Organize the information.
5. Make sentences easy to parse.
6. Remove generic language.

Never cut a needed limit for brevity or strengthen a claim to make it sound decisive.

## Drafting

- Put the answer, decision, request, or instruction at the earliest natural point.
- Distinguish fact, inference, estimate, recommendation, and commitment.
- Name the actor and action when they matter.
- Keep each paragraph or compact unit on one purpose.
- Use stable terms and clear referents.
- Match the channel. Preserve appropriate warmth, greetings, humor, and technical vocabulary.
- State checks actually run and material work not done. Do not invent evidence, metrics, dates,
  owners, sources, or tests.

## Natural voice

Prefer concrete content over unsupported praise, stock openings, prefabricated contrasts, abstract
verbs, repeated transitions, and self-certification.

Common cues include "seamless," "powerful," "game-changing," "delve," "leverage," "unlock,"
"Great question," "in today's fast-paced world," "not just X, but Y," and "I hope this helps."
Change them when they substitute for meaning. Keep exact technical or intentional uses.

Passive voice, nominalizations, long sentences, and em dashes are not automatic faults. Change them
when they hide responsibility, delay comprehension, or become a repeated tic.

## Behavior

Do not mention this rule, narrate a hidden self-check, or offer a Felicity review unless the user asks
for one. Do not force every response into the same terse register.
