# Felicity Writing Standard

Use this standard to draft and revise prose. Apply judgment: a rule that makes the text less accurate,
less natural, or less useful does not apply.

## Order of Operations

Make decisions in this order:

1. preserve truth and intended meaning;
2. perform the text's actual job;
3. fit the reader and context;
4. organize the information;
5. improve sentence clarity;
6. remove generic or mechanical language.

Do not trade a higher item for a lower one. Concision never justifies deleting a needed limit, and
natural voice never justifies strengthening a claim.

## Pragmatic Fit

Treat the draft as an action in context. The reader should be able to tell what the writer asserts,
asks, recommends, or commits to; what background is assumed; and what each qualifier limits. Give
enough relevant support for the reader's task without explaining shared context or turning a narrow
fact into a broad implication. Keep a stable topic and clear referents so each sentence follows from
the last. These checks come before a surface polish pass.

## Claims and Reasoning

- Separate observation, inference, estimate, and recommendation when the distinction matters.
- State what a load-bearing conclusion rests on.
- Do not turn sequence into causation.
- Match qualifiers to the evidence: one accurate qualifier is better than stacked hedges.
- State a limit when the reader needs it to act correctly.
- Do not narrate checking or revision when a citation or short qualifier carries the same value.
- Never invent a source, test, metric, date, owner, or unperformed step.
- In a rewrite, preserve every factual, causal, temporal, and evaluative claim in substance. Do not
  add motives, inevitability, or connective rationale such as "this will not fix itself" unless the
  source states it.

Working notes may retain audit detail. External prose should carry the conclusion and the limits its
reader needs, not the history of how the draft was corrected.

## Purpose and Force

- Put the answer, decision, request, or recommendation at the earliest natural point.
- State the desired next action.
- Distinguish requirement, suggestion, estimate, and commitment.
- Keep supporting detail proportional to the decision it informs.
- Use headings that communicate the point, not generic labels, when the genre permits.

Chronological narratives, sensitive messages, and persuasive arguments may need context before the
main point. Use the reader's needs, not a fixed opening formula.

## Audience and Register

- Write for the reader's knowledge, relationship, and stakes.
- Define unfamiliar terms once.
- Keep technical terms when they are more exact than a plain substitute.
- Preserve greetings, warmth, humor, and directness appropriate to the genre.
- Do not translate every voice into terse corporate prose.
- A public README, customer document, or shared deck is external prose even when it lives in a
  repository.

## Structure

- Give each paragraph one controlling purpose.
- Connect consecutive sentences through a shared subject, referent, or logical step.
- Put one main claim in each slide or compact unit.
- Group comparable list items and use parallel grammar.
- Keep on-slide copy concise; move supporting detail to notes.
- Put important conditions near the statement they limit.

Vary sentence rhythm naturally. Do not repeat a subject merely to satisfy a mechanical coherence
test.

## Sentences

- Prefer identifiable actors and concrete verbs.
- Place modifiers next to what they modify.
- Use one stable name for each important object.
- Break a sentence when its clauses perform different jobs.
- Cut words that add no meaning.
- Choose punctuation by relationship: period for separation, colon for explanation, comma for close
  connection, parentheses for a true aside.

Passive voice, nominalizations, long sentences, and em dashes can all be right. Change them when they
hide the actor, delay comprehension, or become a repeated tic.

## Natural Voice

Replace generic language with concrete content:

- unsupported praise: "powerful," "world-class," "game-changing";
- stock openings: "Great question," "In today's fast-paced world";
- stock contrasts: "not just X, but Y";
- abstract verbs where a plain verb is more exact: "delve," "leverage," "unlock," "utilize";
- ceremonial transitions repeated for cadence;
- padded triads whose final item adds no idea;
- self-certification: "every claim is verified," "this is not a guess";
- revision narration: "as corrected above," "in the earlier draft";
- automatic closers: "I hope this helps."

These are context-sensitive signals. Keep genuine technical senses such as "robust to noise,"
"resource utilization," "financial leverage," or a named "deep dive." Keep a familiar idiom when it
fits the speaker better than a bloodless substitute.

After a rewrite, compare the draft sentence by sentence with the source. A smoother sentence is not
an improvement if it adds a claim the author did not make.

## Text-Specific Guidance

### Email, post, and message

- Open in the register people actually use in that channel.
- State the news or ask early.
- Make owner and timing explicit when action is required.
- End on the useful last line, not a reflex offer to help.

### Report, README, and design document

- Start sections with the conclusion or purpose readers need.
- Keep evidence near the claim it supports.
- Preserve verification status and unresolved decisions.
- Separate current state, recommendation, and next action.

### Deck

- Use assertion titles when the slide supports an assertion.
- Keep one main claim per slide.
- Write slide copy for scanning and notes for explanation.
- Do not force full sentences into labels or diagrams.

### Prompt and system prompt

- State task, constraints, and output shape.
- Prefer a positive target alongside any prohibition.
- Define ambiguous terms and priority between conflicting instructions.
- Include an example only when it clarifies the intended range.

### Tool or skill description

- Lead with what it does and the user situation that should trigger it.
- Use words users are likely to say.
- State the boundary against the nearest sibling.
- Describe actual behavior, not aspirations or implementation detail.
